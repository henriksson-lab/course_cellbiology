# Expected learning outcomes

A first-cycle course (**3MB001**, Umeå University) in modern molecular cell
biology for year-2 biomedicine students. The verbatim outcomes are in
[courseplan.md](courseplan.md). This page says what each outcome means and where it
is taught.

## Overall course outcome

By the end of the course, a student can **follow a molecule, a signal or a cell
decision through the cell**: where a protein is made, how it reaches its
compartment, how the cell senses and answers a signal, how it moves, divides and
dies. They can also **explain how a drug or a disease acts on that path.**

## Theory outcomes (ELO 1–9: written exam, one section each)

Each ELO is examined separately with a 60 % pass bar and one VG question, so each
needs enough lectures, quiz questions and study questions of its own.

| ELO | Short name | What a student can do | Taught in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cell & methods | Describe the parts of a eukaryotic cell and choose a method (microscopy, fractionation, flow cytometry, perturbation) to answer a cell biology question | The cell and how we study it; a methods slide in every lecture |
| 2 | Membranes & transport | Explain bilayer structure and fluidity, membrane proteins, passive and active transport, channels and membrane potential | Membranes and transport |
| 3 | Signalling | Describe signal types, receptor classes, second messengers, kinase cascades, feedback and how signals are switched off | Cell signalling |
| 4 | Protein handling | Describe protein folding, sorting, translocation, modification, quality control and degradation | Protein handling and organelles |
| 5 | Organelles & homeostasis | Compare organelles' structure and function, and explain how they cooperate to keep the cell in balance (energy, Ca²⁺, pH, lipids, protein load) | Protein handling and organelles (especially mitochondria and organelles compared) |
| 6 | Cytoskeleton & motility | Explain actin, microtubules and intermediate filaments, motors, migration and transport, and give examples of methods to study them | Cytoskeleton |
| 7 | Cell cycle & division | Describe the cell-cycle control system, checkpoints, mitosis and cytokinesis, and control of cell numbers | Cell cycle and division |
| 8 | Cell death | Describe apoptosis (intrinsic and extrinsic), necroptosis, pyroptosis and ferroptosis, and their regulation | Cell death |
| 9 | Disease mechanisms | Explain disease as cell biology going wrong: cancer, misfolding, trafficking, channel and mitochondrial defects | Disease mechanisms, plus every lecture's "Why it matters" slides |

The final lecture follows one drug through the cell and revisits all nine ELOs.

## Skills outcomes (ELO 10–19)

These are taught and assessed in the labs and in module 2 (oral presentations,
seminars, group problem solving, poster), which are handled outside this site.

- **ELO 10–12, 17–19** → Module 2 (PPT): oral presentation, group problem solving,
  discussion, poster for a commissioning party, projects as a working method,
  own learning needs.
- **ELO 13–16** → Module 3 (Lab): planning and running experiments, GLP, written
  report, interpreting results.

## Assessment support on this site

- **Quiz** per lecture: short self-check.
- **Study questions** per lecture (replacing the programming exercises of the
  scaffold course): G-level recall and explanation questions and **one VG-style
  question** ("predict what happens if…", "compare…", "design an experiment to…"),
  written the way the exam asks them.
