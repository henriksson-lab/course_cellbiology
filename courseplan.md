# Course syllabus: 3MB001

Summary of the official syllabus. The syllabus itself is the binding version:
<https://www.umu.se/en/education/syllabus/3mb001/>

## Course identification

- **English title:** Cell Biology
- **Swedish title:** Cellbiologi
- **Course code:** 3MB001 (replaces the earlier 3MB038 *Cellbiologi*)
- **Credits:** 15 ECTS
- **Education level:** First cycle
- **Main fields of study:** Molecular Biology; Biomedical Sciences (both first
  cycle, less than 60 credits of first-cycle courses as entry requirements)
- **Grading scale:** Three-grade scale (U / G / VG)
- **Responsible department:** Department of Molecular Biology
- **Established:** by PRBMT, 2025-10-27
- **Validity:** 2027-01-18 and until further notice
- **First run:** spring 2027, 18 January to 24 March 2027, on campus in Umeå,
  taught in English

## Modules

1. **Theory.** Fundamental theory in modern molecular cell biology. *This site
   covers this module.*
2. **PPT: Working Life and Career.** Oral presentations, seminar discussions,
   problem solving, a poster on a biomedical problem for a commissioning party,
   and projects as a working method.
3. **Laboratory Work.** Cell biology methods: planning and running experiments,
   analysing and presenting data, writing results in scientific form.

## Expected learning outcomes (verbatim)

**Knowledge and Understanding.** The student should be able to:

1. Describe the structure and function of the cell and explain methods used to study cell biological processes.
2. Explain the structure and function of cell membranes and the principles of membrane transport.
3. Describe fundamental principles of cell communication and signal transduction.
4. Describe how proteins are handled and regulated in the cell.
5. Compare the structure and function of different organelles and explain their roles in cellular homeostasis.
6. Explain the basis of cell shape, motility, and intracellular transport, and give examples of methods used to study these processes.
7. Describe the regulation of the cell cycle and the mechanisms of cell division.
8. Describe different forms of programmed cell death and their regulation.
9. Explain basic concepts of cell biological disease mechanisms.

**Skills and Abilities.** The student should be able to:

10. Present scientific content orally in a clear and structured manner.
11. Collaborate in groups to solve cell biological problems and communicate the results effectively.
12. Participate in structured scientific discussions and critically reflect on theoretical and practical content.
13. Plan and conduct basic experiments in cell biology.
14. Apply good laboratory practice and follow established protocols.
15. Analyze and present experimental data in written form.
16. Interpret results and draw conclusions based on scientific principles.
17. Demonstrate skills and the ability to identify, formulate, and propose solutions to a problem relevant to the education, based on labor market needs
18. Demonstrate the ability to define and describe projects as a working method.

**Judgement and Approach.** The student should be able to:

19. Identify one's own learning needs and take responsibility for further learning

## Entry requirements

20 ECTS in chemistry, of which at least 5 ECTS in biochemistry (or 20 ECTS in
chemistry combined with physics and/or mathematics, of which at least 5 ECTS in
biochemistry); 20 ECTS in biology, including microbiology, molecular biology and
genetics.

## Instruction

Lectures plus student-centred teaching: interactive seminars, case-based learning
and practical work. **All teaching except lectures is mandatory.** Lab work
requires knowing the lab safety rules and basic GLP. Taught in English (Swedish
if no participant needs English).

## Examination

| Module | Form | ELOs | Grades |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Theory | Individual written exam | 1–9 | U / G / VG |
| 2 PPT | Individual oral presentation | 10–12, 17–19 | U / G |
| 3 Lab | Written lab report | 13–16 | U / G |

**How the written exam is built (this shapes the course material):**

- The exam has **one section per ELO (1–9)**. To pass an ELO, at least 60 % of
  its questions must be correct. **All nine ELOs must be passed.**
- **Each ELO has one VG question**, marked G or VG. Exam VG = at least 5 of the 9
  VG questions at VG.
- A student who passes at least 6 ELOs but not the whole exam may take a group
  oral exam (assessed individually) on the failed ELOs, or retake the full exam.
- Course VG = all mandatory parts done + written exam at VG.

## Literature

Not published online. The department uses *Molecular Biology of the Cell*
(Alberts et al.). **Students should not have to read the book to follow this
course**; each lecture ends with optional chapter pointers.
