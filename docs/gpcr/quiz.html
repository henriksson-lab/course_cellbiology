Quiz: G-protein-coupled receptors and second messengers

Short questions to check your understanding of the lecture G-protein-coupled receptors and second messengers. Try each on paper first, then open the hint or the answer. The tag after each question shows the learning outcome (ELO) it practises.

Q1. Adrenaline never enters a liver cell, yet it changes the cell’s metabolism within seconds. What crosses the membrane: the hormone, an ion, or something else? [ELO 3]

Hint

Think about what the receptor protein does when the hormone binds on the outside.

Answer

Nothing crosses. The receptor is a seven-helix membrane protein. Hormone binding outside changes the shape of the helix bundle, which opens a pocket on the cytosolic face. The G protein binds there and is activated. The information crosses the membrane as a change in protein shape.

Q2. What exactly does an active GPCR do to the G protein? Why is it called a GEF? [ELO 3]

Hint

Does the receptor add a phosphate to GDP?

Answer

It opens Gα’s nucleotide pocket so that GDP leaves; GTP, which is about ten times more abundant in the cytosol, then binds. The receptor only catalyses the exchange of guanine nucleotides: a guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF). It does not phosphorylate anything, and after release of Gα–GTP it can activate the next G protein.

Q3. Match each Gα type to its main effect: Gs, Gi, Gq. Effects: (a) activates phospholipase C-β; (b) inhibits adenylyl cyclase; (c) activates adenylyl cyclase. [ELO 3]

Hint

s = stimulatory, i = inhibitory.

Answer

Gs → (c): more cAMP. Gi → (b): less cAMP (its βγ also opens K⁺ channels). Gq → (a): PLC-β cuts PIP₂ into IP₃ and DAG; IP₃ releases Ca²⁺ from the ER.

Q4. How does cAMP activate protein kinase A? How many cAMP molecules are needed to release one catalytic subunit? [ELO 3]

Hint

PKA is R₂C₂. Each R subunit binds two cAMP.

Answer

The regulatory (R) subunits block the catalytic (C) subunits. Four cAMP bind the two R subunits, which change shape and release the two C subunits as active kinases. So two cAMP per C subunit: this step does not amplify. The amplification is at enzyme steps: the receptor, adenylyl cyclase, and the kinases.

Q5. Phospholipase C-β cuts PIP₂ into two products. Name them, say where each goes, and what each does. [ELO 3]

Hint

One product is water-soluble, the other is a lipid.

Answer

IP₃ is water-soluble: it diffuses through the cytosol and opens the IP₃ receptor, a Ca²⁺ channel in the ER membrane, so cytosolic Ca²⁺ rises from about 100 nM to about 1 µM. DAG is a lipid and stays in the plasma membrane, where it activates protein kinase C together with Ca²⁺.

Q6. List four ways the cAMP pathway is switched off after adrenaline has gone. [ELO 3]

Hint

One off-switch for each active molecule: receptor, Gα, cAMP, phosphorylated proteins.

Answer
  1. The hormone dissociates and is removed; active receptors are also phosphorylated by GRK and bound by arrestin (desensitisation) and taken in by endocytosis. (2) Gα hydrolyses GTP to GDP and rejoins βγ. (3) Phosphodiesterases turn cAMP into AMP. (4) Protein phosphatases remove the phosphates that PKA added.

Q7. Cholera toxin modifies Gαs so it cannot hydrolyse GTP. Why does this cause watery diarrhoea? [ELO 3] [ELO 9]

Hint

Follow the chain: Gαs → cAMP → PKA → which chloride channel?

Answer

Once Gαs is switched on, it stays on. Adenylyl cyclase keeps making cAMP, PKA stays active and keeps the CFTR Cl⁻ channel open in the apical membrane of gut cells. Cl⁻ flows into the gut, Na⁺ follows, and water follows by osmosis: litres of fluid are lost. Oral rehydration (salt + glucose, absorbed by SGLT) replaces it.

Q8. Metoprolol is a β-blocker. Is it an agonist or an antagonist? What happens to cAMP in heart muscle cells when adrenaline is present? [ELO 3] [ELO 9]

Hint

It binds the same pocket as adrenaline.

Answer

A competitive antagonist: it occupies the β₁ receptor without activating it, so fewer receptors are bound by adrenaline. Less Gs is activated, cAMP and PKA activity stay lower, and the heart beats more slowly and with less force. Because it is competitive, very high adrenaline levels can partly overcome the block.
