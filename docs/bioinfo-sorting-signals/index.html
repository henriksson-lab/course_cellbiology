Bioinformatics: read the zip code

Goal: read the sorting signals of real proteins in databases, and map them for 14 proteins in R. Builds on the lectures protein sorting, the ER, the Golgi and endocytosis and lysosomes. About 2 hours, no logins.

Part A: in the browser

UniProt: the LDL receptor

Open LDLR, UniProt P01130. Its signal peptide (residues 1–21) is listed under PTM/Processing. Now click Subcellular Location in the left menu and scroll to its Features table: one Transmembrane segment and two Topological domains.

T1. Which end of the mature receptor is outside the cell: the N-terminal part or the C-terminal part?

Answer (checked 2026-11-19)

The N-terminal part: topological domain 22–788 is Extracellular, the transmembrane segment is 789–810, and 811–860 is Cytoplasmic. LDLR is a type I protein: signal peptide in, stop-transfer segment in the membrane. The ER lumen side becomes the outside of the cell.

UniProt: PCSK9

Open PCSK9, UniProt Q8NBP7. It has a signal peptide (1–30) and no transmembrane segment, so it is secreted. That is why an antibody in the blood can catch it. Click Disease & Variants in the left menu.

T2. Which disease does UniProt link to PCSK9?

Answer (checked 2026-11-19)

Hypercholesterolemia, familial, 3 (FHCL3), autosomal dominant. Gain-of-function PCSK9 sends more LDL receptors to the lysosome. LDLR itself (T1) is linked to FHCL1.

UniProt: BiP, an ER chaperone

Open HSPA5 (BiP), UniProt P11021. It has a signal peptide (1–18), yet it stays in the ER. Click Sequence in the left menu and read the end of the sequence.

T3. What are the last four amino acids of BiP?

Answer (checked 2026-11-19)

KDEL (Lys-Asp-Glu-Leu). BiP leaks to the Golgi with other cargo; the KDEL receptor in the cis-Golgi brings it back to the ER in COPI vesicles.

STRING: partners of LDLR

Open the human LDLR network in STRING. Each node is a protein; each line is a predicted functional link, from experiments, databases or co-mentions in papers. Click a node to see its name.

T4. PCSK9 is in the network. Which one of these four is also there: APOB, GAPDH, CANX or TFRC?

Answer (checked 2026-11-19)

APOB, the protein of the LDL particle that LDLR binds. A STRING line means “these proteins work together”, not necessarily that they touch.

QuickGO: where is CFTR?

Gene Ontology (GO) gives each location a term and each annotation an evidence code. Open QuickGO: CFTR annotations to endoplasmic reticulum membrane (GO:0005789). Find the row whose reference is PMID:11707463.

T5. Which evidence code does that row have, and what does it stand for?

Answer (checked 2026-11-19)

IDA, Inferred from Direct Assay: an experiment located CFTR in the ER membrane. Other rows say IEA (electronic annotation) or TAS (traceable author statement). Misfolded CFTR ΔF508 is held in the ER, as in the ER lecture.

Part B: in R

Download the template bioinfo-sorting-signals.Rmd into a new folder and open it in RStudio. You need the packages rmarkdown, ggplot2 and dplyr. Knit it once as it is: it fetches 14 proteins from UniProt, and the two blanks show as errors. If UniProt is down, set use_snapshot <- TRUE.

Snapshot: uniprot-features-2026-11-19.csv (put it in a data subfolder). Data: UniProtKB release 2026_04 (CC BY 4.0), retrieved 2026-11-19.

All fetching and cleaning code is given. You fill two blanks marked ___:

  1. Chunk kdel: the filter value, the four letters of the ER retrieval signal, in quotes. Expected result: a table with two proteins, both in the ER group.
  2. Chunk feature-map: the aes() mapping. Put feature on x, gene on y and present as fill. Expected result: a tile plot with three columns (signal peptide, transit peptide, transmembrane) and one row per protein, grouped by destination. Dark tiles mean “annotated”.

T6. Your KDEL table shows BiP (HSPA5). Which other protein ends in KDEL?

Answer (checked 2026-11-19)

P4HB (protein disulfide isomerase, PDI), the ER enzyme that forms disulfide bonds.

T7. [VG] In your plot, one cell-surface protein has a transmembrane segment but no signal peptide. Which one, and what kind of segment brings it to the ER?

  • A. LDLR, a stop-transfer segment
  • B. TFRC, a signal-anchor
  • C. TFRC, a mitochondrial transit peptide
  • D. LDLR, a KDEL signal
Answer (checked 2026-11-19)

B. The transferrin receptor (TFRC) is a type II protein: its single hydrophobic segment is both the ER-targeting signal and the membrane anchor, so it has no cleaved signal peptide. UniProt calls it “Signal-anchor for type II membrane protein”. Only the two mitochondrial proteins (ATP5F1A, CS) have transit peptides.

Hand-in checklist

  • Answers to T1–T7.
  • The knitted HTML of your report, with both blanks filled and no errors left.
  • Your plot shows 14 genes and three feature columns.
  • The Data section names the source, the UniProt release and the date.
