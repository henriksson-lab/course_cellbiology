Bioinformatics: which genes does a cancer cell need?

Goal: read CRISPR screens to see which cell-cycle and survival genes cancer cells cannot live without. Builds on cell-cycle control, mitosis, cell numbers and apoptosis. About 2 hours.

Part A: in the browser

A1. Cyclin B1 rises and falls. Open Human Protein Atlas: CCNB1, subcellular section. Scroll to Single cell variation and find Protein expression peak phase.

T1. In which phase is cyclin B1 protein highest: G1, S or G2? [ELO 7]

Answer

G2. Cyclin B1 builds up before mitosis and activates CDK1. The APC/C then marks it for destruction at anaphase, so the cell can leave mitosis.

A2. Who switches p53 on? Open UniProt P04637 (TP53). Go to PTM/Processing and find the Modified residue at position 15.

T2. Which of the kinases listed for serine 15 is the DNA-damage kinase from the checkpoint slide? [ELO 7]

Answer

ATM. After a DNA break, ATM phosphorylates p53 at serine 15. MDM2 can then no longer tag p53 for destruction, p53 accumulates and switches on p21.

A3. A drug against a survival protein. Open ChEMBL CHEMBL3137309 (venetoclax) and find Mechanisms of Action.

T3. Which protein does venetoclax inhibit: BCL-2, BCL-XL, MCL-1 or CDK4? [ELO 8]

Answer

BCL-2 (“Apoptosis regulator Bcl-2 inhibitor”). It is the BH3 mimetic from the apoptosis lecture, used in chronic lymphocytic leukaemia.

Part B: in R

A CRISPR screen knocks out one gene in each of about 1 250 cancer cell lines and measures growth. The gene effect is 0 when the cells grow as usual and about −1 for a typical essential gene. More negative means the cells need the gene more.

The template fetches these scores for six genes: CDK1, CDK4, CDK6 (cell cycle) and BCL2, BCL2L1, MCL1 (BCL-2 family guardians). All fetching code is given. You fill three blanks marked ___.

Downloads

  1. Report template. Put it in a new folder.
  2. Data snapshot (optional). Put it in a data/ subfolder. It is used only if you set use_snapshot <- TRUE (for example when the API is down).

Data: DepMap CRISPR screens via the Open Targets Platform 26.09 (CC0; cite DepMap), retrieved 2026-11-18.

You need the R packages rmarkdown, ggplot2 and httr (install.packages(c("rmarkdown", "ggplot2", "httr"))). Knit once before you change anything: the report knits, with an error under each blank.

Blank 1: one summary line

Replace ___ in FUN = ___ with the R function that computes a median. When it works, a table with six rows prints: one median gene effect per gene.

T4. Which gene has the most negative median, and what is that median (two decimals)? [ELO 7]

Answer

CDK1, about −2.63. Almost every cell line needs CDK1 to enter mitosis. The other five medians lie between −1 and 0 (BCL2 is close to 0).

Blank 2: one ggplot() call

Write one ggplot() call from the pieces listed in the template: data dependencies, gene on the x axis, gene_effect on the y axis, geom_violin(), a dashed line at −1, and axis labels. You should see six violins, one per gene.

T5. Which statement matches your plot? [ELO 8]

    1. BCL2 is needed by most cell lines.
    1. Most cell lines do not need BCL2, but a few lie below −1.
    1. BCL2 and CDK1 have similar violins.
Answer

(b). The BCL2 violin is wide at 0 with a thin tail down to below −2. CDK1 sits almost entirely below −1.

Blank 3: one gene name

Fill in "BCL2" (with quotes) to keep only the BCL2 rows. A plot with one row of points per tissue appears, and below it a count of cell lines with gene effect below −1 per tissue.

T6. Which tissue group has the most cell lines below −1 for BCL2? [ELO 8, 9]

Answer

Lymphoid tissue (5 lines; haematopoietic tissue is next with 2). These are lymphoma, myeloma and leukaemia lines, which fits venetoclax being a drug for blood cancers.

T7. [VG] CDK1 is the strongest dependency in your plot. Why is it a poorer drug target than BCL2? Pick one. [ELO 7, 9]

    1. CDK1 is not expressed in cancer cells.
    1. Normal dividing cells, such as bone marrow and gut lining, also need CDK1, so blocking it would harm them too.
    1. Knocking out CDK1 makes cells grow faster.
Answer

(b). A gene that every dividing cell needs gives little room between killing the cancer and harming the patient. This is why antimitotic drugs lower blood counts (mitosis lecture). BCL2 is needed by only a few kinds of cancer cell, so a BCL-2 inhibitor can be more selective.

Hand in

  • The knitted HTML of your report, with no Error messages left.
  • It shows the table of six medians, the six-gene violin plot and the BCL2 plot by tissue.
  • Your answers to T1–T7, one line each.
